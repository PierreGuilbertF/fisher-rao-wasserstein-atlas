Location--dispersion transformations of an asymmetric reference density. Starting from the positive profile q(x) = 0.8 exp(-(x+0.8)²/(2·0.45²)) + 0.25 exp(-(x-1.5)²/(2·0.65²)), we compute its normalization, mean, and standard deviation by numerical quadrature and transform it into a probability density p of mean zero and variance one. This standardization preserves its asymmetry. The four panels display (a) p = p_{0,1}, (b) the shifted density p_{1.6,1}, (c) the enlarged density p_{0,1.6}, and (d) the shifted and enlarged density p_{1.6,1.6}, where p_{mu,sigma}(x) = sigma^{-1}p((x-mu)/sigma). All panels use identical axis limits. Dotted vertical lines mark the means mu; dashed gray curves repeat p for comparison in the transformed panels. Increasing sigma stretches the horizontal scale and reduces density heights by the factor 1/sigma, preserving total probability. Because p is standardized, the mean and standard deviation of each transformed density are exactly mu and sigma. The reference shape is fixed across the four panels; only its location and dispersion change.
